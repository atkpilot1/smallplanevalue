## The narrative (use this everywhere)

Most instrument students can fly an approach when the plate is already loaded and ATC cooperates.

Real IFR is the next thirty seconds: a new approach, a hold, and a clean entry into the segment.

We flew John Tune to Morristown, Morristown to Huntington, and home to Tune. Three airports. Multiple approach changes at each one. Arrivals and departure procedures into Morristown. Holds — and the best way to join the approach after the hold. Then we turned on the flight director and flew it that way.

That is instrument training that shows up on a real clearance, not just in the practice area.

5. The flight director isn’t cheating. It’s how you stay ahead of the airplane when ATC changes the plan.

## What to film (phone is enough)

1. Approach plate going from one procedure to another (over the shoulder, no student face unless they agree).
2. Flight director / command bars coming alive.
3. Hold on the map or HSI.
4. Three seconds of each airport environment (don’t shoot identifiable faces on the ramp).
5. Hangar 142 / KJWN sign for the closer.

Audio: your voice, not ATC, unless you strip callsigns. Never post a live frequency recording with a student’s tail number if they haven’t agreed.

## What not to say

- Don’t name the student or the company that paid the invoice.
- Don’t promise a rating, a pass, or a timeline.
- Don’t imply this trip meets every FAA instrument-cross-country requirement for every student — it was training, not a sign-off post.
- Don’t argue with other CFIs in the comments. Offer a lesson, not a debate.
