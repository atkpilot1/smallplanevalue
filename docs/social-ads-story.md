# SmallPlaneValue — Instagram, YouTube, Facebook narrative

**Site:** https://smallplanevalue.com  
**Job of this content:** Get a pilot who already has a listing open to run a free valuation.  
**Not the job:** Approach briefings, IFR training, cockpit procedure videos.

## The story (one narrative, three platforms)

**Working title:** *The hangar number*

You’ve already found the airplane. It’s on Controller, Trade-A-Plane, or a type-club thread. The photos look honest. The seller is “firm.” Your CFI buddy says it’s a steal. A forum post says it’s $40k high.

There is no Zillow for Bonanzas. Sale prices in GA are not public. Bluebooks were built for a different market. So most buyers either **pay the ask** or **walk away from a good plane** because they don’t have a number they trust.

SmallPlaneValue is the 60-second hangar number: typical **ask**, **fair market**, and **buyer target** for *this* listing — plus a pre-buy checklist — without inventing sold comps.

**One line:**  
*Find the plane anywhere. Get the number here.*

**Brand promise (say this, don’t say “briefing”):**  
Know what to offer before you call the seller.

## What to film / what to skip

**Film this**

- A phone in a hangar, kitchen, or FBO lounge with a real listing on screen
- N-number, year, airframe time, “fresh annual,” GNS 430 vs glass
- Typing the N-number or pasting the listing into smallplanevalue.com
- The three numbers appearing: ask · fair · buyer target
- A logbook stack, a pre-buy checklist, a handshake at the tiedown — *not* a takeoff roll as the hero shot

**Skip this**

- Approach plates, briefing flows, “five miles from the FAF”
- Headset-cam IFR, pattern work, “watch me fly this RNAV”
- Fake “SOLD for $___” graphics (we don’t fabricate sale prices)
- Trash-talking FindAircraft / Windsock by name on ads (use the gap, not the dunk)

If you want aviation *texture*, use ramp noise, a hangar door, a tail number. Don’t turn the ad into a lesson.

---

## Voice

- Pilot talking to a pilot who’s shopping, not a broker pitching a report
- Concrete: “1979 182, 1,400 SMOH, no damage history” — not “leverage our AI”
- Honest: “We don’t have MLS solds. Nobody does. Here’s the ask range and a negotiation band anyway.”
- Invite correction: “Tell us if the number feels high or low.” That *is* the marketing.

## YouTube

## A 5-post series (same week, all three platforms)

One story, five chapters. Post every other day.

| # | Theme | Hook |
|---|--------|------|
| 1 | The listing | “You found the plane. You still don’t have a number.” |
| 2 | No Zillow | “If a site shows you a GA *sold* price, ask where it came from.” |
| 3 | Live demo | Screen record one real N-number (your plane or a public listing) |
| 4 | Pre-buy = leverage | “The checklist isn’t to scare you. It’s how you talk $8k off.” |
| 5 | Help us calibrate | “Know 182s / A36s / RVs? Tell us if we’re off.” |

Chapter 5 is the trust closer. It also produces testimonials you can quote later (with permission).

## What success looks like

You’re not chasing views from people who want to watch approaches. You’re chasing **pilots with a listing open**.

Watch:

- Clicks to smallplanevalue.com (UTMs above)
- `valuation_completed` in GA4
- Feedback tab “too high / too low / about right”
- Comments like “ran my 182 / my listing” — reply, ask them to try the site

If a video gets lots of watch time but no valuations, it’s entertaining flying content. Recut toward the listing and the three numbers.
